The core identity & platform

NexusHub

The company's employee platform and sign-in provider: records, leave, requests, onboarding and news in one place, plus the identity every other app trusts.

hub.nexus-suite.internal · live showcase
Click to enlarge ⤢
Company feed with a pinned announcement, read tracking and likes Interactive Console

Employee records

Personal data is split into four privacy levels (public, employment, confidential and sensitive). Government IDs are encrypted and every confidential view is logged. Includes CSV import, an org chart and a directory.

Leave

Philippine statutory leave types, balances earned monthly or yearly, service-year steps, carry-over and conversion, a team calendar, iCal feeds and figures for payroll.

Request forms

A drag-and-drop form builder with versions and approval flows. Certificates are generated as PDFs with a letterhead, drawn signatures and a QR code anyone can verify.

Approvals engine

Step-by-step flows (manager, role or person), delegation while away, reminders after two working days and escalation after five. Other apps can use it too.

Onboarding & offboarding

Checklist templates on a timeline, a pre-hire portal for uploading requirements, probation reminders, clearance sign-offs and exit interviews.

Company feed

Posts, photos, mentions and likes. Announcements can be pinned and ask people to confirm they've read them. Includes moderation, birthdays and work anniversaries.

Roles from permissions

HR builds roles by choosing an access level for each module. Nobody can grant more access than they hold, and two-factor sign-in is required for every role.

Sign-in for every app

An OpenID Connect provider with PKCE and consent. Signing out in one app signs you out of the others (back-channel logout), and you can see and remove connected apps.

Attendance sheet

HR's cut-off sheet: time in and out per day, late and undertime from each person's work schedule, leave and onsite requests filled in, and a summary for payroll. Kiosk punches from NexusAttendance fill it in; HR's values always win, and locked cut-offs never change.

My attendance

Employees see their own sheet for the cut-off with an assessment (good, fair, needs attention) and plain tips, and managers see their team's.

Meetings

Meetings for a client and project are asked for in the Hub, approved and confirmed by every attendee, then show up as a task on the project's board in NexusTask.

Team calendar

Who is off this month at a glance, with holidays and requests still waiting marked. Approved leave can follow you into Google or Outlook through a calendar link.

The connected hardware asset tracker

NexusInventory

IT asset inventory across sites: what you own, where it is, who holds it and what it's made of. Employees sign in through the Hub, and the employee list stays in sync with the Hub automatically.

inv.nexus-suite.internal · asset console
Click to enlarge ⤢

Assets & components

Laptops, servers, network gear, point-of-sale devices and parts. Each asset type has its own specs, and a server's specs are worked out from the parts installed in it.

Assign, lend & acknowledge

Assign equipment or lend it with a due date, then take it back. The holder acknowledges what they received, and every change is kept in the asset's history.

QR labels & scanning

Print labels as PDF or for Zebra printers, scan them with a phone camera, and reissue a code if a label is lost. Large print batches run in the background.

Purchase orders

Draft, approve (by a different site admin), order, then receive straight into stock, with documents attached.

Sites, stock & stocktake

Sites, floors, rooms and storage areas, with access limited to each person's site. Bulk stock, transfers between sites, stocktake by scanning and disposal with proof the drives were wiped.

Offboarding clearance

When someone resigns, the Hub's checklist asks NexusInventory what they still hold, and the clearance task signs off by itself once everything is returned.

Clients & RMAs

Client orders staged and delivered with a signature, client-owned equipment tracked over time, and returns for repair (RMA).

Reports & depreciation

Accountability certificates, site registers, warranties expiring, valuation and replacement planning, plus CSV and Excel exports and scheduled reports.

Precision delivery management

NexusProject

Projects, their teams, clients and milestones, with progress that comes from the real work in NexusTask instead of someone's estimate. Status movement, an on-time or late timetable and delivery reports show where every project really stands.

Projects & teams

Each project has a code, status, priority, lead, client and dates. Members get a project role, and a project is visible only to its members and project admins.

Milestones

Delivery points in order, each with a due date and status. Tasks in NexusTask are filed under the open ones.

Progress from NexusTask

Tasks stay in NexusTask. NexusProject keeps a copy from NexusTask's task events, so the percentage done and the done, in progress and to do counts are always current.

Overview

Active projects, your projects, milestones at risk, overdue tasks and the last seven days of movement on one page.

Status movement

Every status change from NexusTask, a cumulative flow chart per project, each task's history and the tasks that are stuck.

Timetable

Milestone lanes per project and a portfolio timeline: on time, late, overdue or on track, with milestones as finish lines.

Delivery reports

Delivery health, open work per person and throughput, by project or department, printable or as CSV.

Clients, vendors & meetings

The suite's client and vendor list with contacts, and the Hub's meetings on each project with figures and advice.

Fluid collaborative productivity

NexusTask

Your tasks and your team's in one place: personal and team lists, boards, due dates and reminders. Tasks for a project are filed under it, and NexusProject sees their progress.

task.nexus-suite.internal · board view

My tasks & Today

A work queue sorted by due date, priority or list, a priority mix, this week's done count and a quick add. Today lines up what's overdue, due, pinned and done around a progress ring.

Lists & boards

A personal list plus team lists you share. Drag tasks across the board's columns, or switch to a list with filters for status, person, priority and due date.

Company workflow

Admins set the statuses every task moves through, in board order. Each task gets a number (T-12) that you can search for.

Project tasks

File a task under a NexusProject project and milestone, and it's numbered by the project code (ENG-4). The project's team decides who can do what.

Calendar & saved views

A month and week calendar where you drag a task to another day. Search, then save a view so it stays in the sidebar.

Comments & files

Comments with @mentions, an activity history and attachments up to 10 MB. Files are stored outside the web root and shown only to people who can open the task.

Notifications

Hub notifications when you're assigned or mentioned or a task you follow changes, plus 08:00 reminders for tasks due tomorrow or overdue.

On the Hub home page

A My tasks card on NexusHub's home page asks NexusTask for your open tasks live, using a short-lived service token from the Hub.

Face clock-in at the door

NexusAttendance

A tablet at the office entrance recognises an enrolled employee's face, asks for a blink and records the punch: no cards, no fingerprints, nothing in hand. Punches flow to NexusHub's attendance sheet, where HR keeps the final say.

attendance.nexus-suite.internal · kiosk
Click to enlarge ⤢
The kiosk at the lobby, waiting for a face Interactive Console

Look, blink, done

The kiosk checks about ten times a second for exactly one face, matches it, then waits for a blink. The first punch of the day is In, later ones are Out.

Only the right person

A match must be close and clearly ahead of the next-best person. A printed photo can't blink, the same person within two minutes is "Already recorded", and three misses send them to HR.

Enrollment with consent

An attendance admin records the signed biometric consent, then captures five samples. A face already enrolled as someone else is refused.

Privacy by design

Face templates are sensitive personal information under RA 10173: encrypted at rest, never logged or sent in events, every enrollment, view and removal audited, and deleted when the Hub says someone resigned.

Paired kiosks

A tablet pairs once with a single-use code that lasts ten minutes, then holds a device cookie. A kiosk can record punches and nothing else, and revoking it locks it out at once.

Punch review

Admins filter punches by date, site, kiosk or person, see how strong each match was, open the snapshot (logged), export CSV, and void a wrong punch with a reason.

Into the Hub's sheet

Each punch is written to the client kit's outbox in the same transaction and sent to NexusHub, which fills in time in and out. It never overwrites what HR typed, and locked cut-offs stay locked.

The Hub decides

NexusAttendance owns kiosks, faces and raw punches. Work schedules, late and undertime, cut-offs and the payroll file stay in NexusHub, so there is one source of truth.

System Architecture

One hub, any number of apps

Apps connect only to the Hub and never share secrets with each other. A shared client kit (a Symfony bundle) gives each new app all of this in an afternoon. NexusTask and NexusProject talk through it too: task events go out through the Hub, and service tokens let each read the other's API. NexusAttendance sends every kiosk punch to the Hub the same way, and the Hub turns it into HR's attendance sheet.

Quality gates on every commit

  • PHPStan level 8, with no baseline and no ignored errors
  • PHPUnit integration and web tests against real MySQL
  • PHP-CS-Fixer, linting and Doctrine schema checks
  • Hand-written, reversible database migrations
  • Contract tests: NexusProject checks itself against NexusTask's task event fixtures
  • An interactive installer sets up one AlmaLinux or Rocky server with the Hub and the chosen apps, already connected

Privacy & security

  • Privacy levels enforced in one place, with a log of every view of confidential data
  • Secrets encrypted at rest, with key rotation
  • Strict content security policy, CSRF protection, and login throttling and lockout
  • Employees can download their own data, and accept versioned privacy notices
  • Reviewed against OWASP ASVS level 2
